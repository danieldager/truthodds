Paper, Loop, Urn

Three systems on the AVeriTeC dev 500, one gold set, matched conventions · comparison generated 2026-09-07

01What is being compared

ClaimCheck reports 0.764 four-class accuracy on the AVeriTeC dev 500. We ran two systems of our own on the same 500 gold rows, which are 491 unique claims with the nine duplicate rows inheriting their claim's prediction. The first is our verify loop, run one claim at a time. The second is the log-odds urn scoring the same claims with weights frozen from our own fact-check gold, no refit anywhere on this page. Both run the same model. Both cap evidence at the claim's own date and drop the outlet the claim came from. Intervals are 95 percent percentile bootstraps over the gold rows, 1,000 resamples, seed 0.

Two conventions run through everything below. Four-class accuracy uses the ClaimCheck convention, where our unsupported verdict counts as Refuted. Every other number uses binary map A, where gold Supported passes and every other gold class flags.

The gap to the paper is mostly a date ceiling. ClaimCheck sends its ceiling to Google as a DD/MM/YYYY string, which Google reads month first, so on 282 of the 500 rows the day is past 12, the string is not a valid date and the filter is dropped, which is 56.4% of the set running with no ceiling at all. We send the correct form and Google honours it, and even then 15.4% of the 843 Serper searches in our top 3 arm came back with at least one page published after the claim's date.

03The urn ranks these claims as well as it ranks ours

The urn's AUC here is 0.868 for the 7-flag model, against 0.862 on the fact-check gold it was fitted on. The 3-voice model gives 0.847 here against 0.852 there. A score fitted on one corpus ranks a different corpus just as well.

The fitted operating point does not transfer, and it was never meant to. At the threshold fitted for a 2 percent false alarm budget on our own feed, the urn flags 47.1% of AVeriTeC's flaggable claims at a 4.1% false positive rate. AVeriTeC is not our feed. The ranking is the transferable part, so the rest of this page compares at matched false positive rates.

06What this says

ClaimCheck's 0.764 is partly a product of three protocol choices, two of them mistakes. The date filter it sends to Google is malformed, so on 56.4% of the claims the search runs with no ceiling and the fact check article about the claim is available as evidence. Absence of evidence is scored as Refuted on a split that is 61.0% Refuted. And there is no corroboration rule, so a single page closes a claim. Our loop keeps the ceiling, keeps the rule, and lands at 0.720. Correct for their two mistakes and it lands at 0.758, the same performance. On the decision we actually ship, flag or pass, the loop is the stronger instrument at every operating point we measured. The frozen urn comes within 4.5 points of the loop's recall at the same false positive rate, with one search stage instead of up to 3. That makes the urn a usable first pass and the loop the thing you escalate to.

  • The date ceiling is a filter with leaks. 15.4% of the 843 Serper searches in the top 3 arm returned at least one post-claim page, and its 318 Exa searches carry no date instrumentation at all.
  • The urn's weights were fitted on our own fact-check gold, 3,274 claims, and were frozen before this run. Nothing here was fitted on AVeriTeC.
  • Binary map A carries the page. On map B the 7-flag urn edges ahead instead, 0.941 against 0.935 on top 3 and 0.909 against 0.885 on all 10, and map C keeps the ordering.
  • Reading all ten documents is not measurably better than reading three. McNemar gives p=0.892 on four-class and p=0.175 on binary A.
  • The paper's number has no interval and no per-item predictions, so it cannot enter a paired test against either of ours.
