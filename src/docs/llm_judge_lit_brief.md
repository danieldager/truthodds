# LLM-as-Judge for Gold-Label-Free Pipeline Evaluation — Literature Brief

Scope: evaluating a fact-checking pipeline's verdicts with LLM-judge panels + sparse human adjudication, no gold labels. Prioritizing 2024–2026 work. Every claim below is drawn from a source I fetched or read in search; arXiv IDs given. Treat 2026 IDs as preprints.

## Key findings

- **Judges agree with humans well on preference, worse on knowledge-intensive verdicts.** GPT-4-class judges hit ~80% agreement with humans on chat preference, but on expert/factual domains agreement drops to ~60–68%. Grounded-verdict tasks are exactly the harder regime for judges. (survey/emergentmind roundups; corroborated across sources)
- **SAFE (search-augmented factuality) matched humans 72% of the time and *beat* them on 76% of a 100-case disagreement audit, at ~20× lower cost.** Evidence that a well-scaffolded judge (decompose → search → adjudicate atomic facts) can be *more* reliable than crowd humans on factuality — but failure tracks search-result quality and reasoning errors. (Wei et al., NeurIPS 2024, arXiv:2403.18802)
- **FactScore** decomposes generations into atomic facts and scores % supported by a source; its automated estimator tracks human FactScore within <2% error — but scores are sensitive to the decomposition strategy and gameable by repeated trivial facts. (Min et al., EMNLP 2023, arXiv:2305.14251)
- **Ev2R** is the current fact-checking-specific reference evaluator: LLM-as-judge over atomic-decomposed evidence, higher human correlation and adversarial robustness than symbolic/proxy scoring. Adopted by the **AVeriTeC 2025 shared task** (Llama 3.3 70B grader; evidence counted correct at Ev2R recall ≥ 0.44). (Akhtar et al., arXiv:2411.05375)
- **Documented judge biases are systematic, not noise:** position bias (verdict flips on answer-order swap; direction is model-dependent), verbosity/length bias (longer = preferred), and self-enhancement. "Justice or Prejudice" quantifies ~12 bias types. (arXiv:2410.02736)
- **Self-preference is real and tied to self-recognition — and it survives blinding.** Judges favor their own generations; strength correlates linearly with self-recognition ability (NeurIPS 2024). **Family bias** (e.g., a Claude judge over-rating Claude outputs) compounds beyond single-model self-preference, and the effect persists even when authorship is hidden. (arXiv:2410.21819; corroborating self-preference work)
- **Sycophancy / confirmation bias in judging:** RLHF-tuned models ratify a stated hypothesis or existing answer rather than falsifying it; chain-of-thought can *amplify* early commitments rather than correct them. Judges also respond to implicit shortcut cues (e.g., "recent" framing) without naming them. Directly relevant to a judge auditing a verdict + its stated rationale. (confirmation-bias/hidden-shortcut studies, arXiv:2602.07996 and related)
- **Panels (PoLL) beat a single large judge — cheaply.** A Panel of LLM Evaluators from *disjoint* model families, pooled by voting/averaging, outperformed a single large judge across 6 datasets / 3 settings with less intra-model bias and >7× lower cost. (Verga et al., 2024, arXiv:2404.18796)
- **But correlated errors cap panel value hard.** A 9-judge / 7-family panel carried only **n_eff ≈ 2.18 independent votes** (24% independence ratio; mean pairwise error correlation ≈0.39). Adding judges past ~5 gave negligible gain; **29% of items had all 9 judges right, 5% all 9 wrong**. Crucially, panels did **not** always beat the best single judge (−6.5 pp on SNLI), and Dawid-Skene / accuracy-weighted aggregation closed ≤11% of the gap *even with gold labels*. Recommendation: report n_eff; distrust results when n_eff/k < 0.5. (arXiv:2605.29800)
- **Pairwise vs absolute is contested.** Consensus: pairwise verdicts align with human preference more reliably than absolute scores. Counter-evidence: "The Comparative Trap" shows pairwise *amplifies* bias — ~20–35% preference flips vs ~9% for absolute scoring under a distractor — and recommends a hybrid. Net: use pairwise for A/B version comparison, but always position-swap and keep an absolute-score backstop. (pro: arXiv:2504.14716; con: arXiv:2406.12319)
- **Bias-corrected prevalence estimation is the right calibration frame.** Judge output is a noisy classifier; correct it with Rogan-Gladen: θ̂ = (p̂ + q̂₀ − 1)/(q̂₀ + q̂₁ − 1), where q̂₀,q̂₁ = judge specificity/sensitivity from a human-labeled calibration set. With sens 0.9 / spec 0.7, **~200 human-labeled calibration items give a CI half-width ~0.1**; spend ~10% of eval budget on it; robust to test/calibration distribution shift. (arXiv:2511.21140)
- **Prediction-Powered Inference (PPI)** is the alternative recipe: combine many cheap judge labels with a small human-labeled sample for an unbiased metric with valid, tighter CIs; extensions add power-tuning, stratification, active/cost-optimal sampling. Use when you can assume calibration and test come from the same distribution. (PPI-for-eval line, e.g., arXiv:2606.05308, 2601.05420)

## Design implications for this team's audit battery

- **Judge count & diversity:** 3–5 judges from genuinely *disjoint* families (e.g., one each Anthropic / OpenAI / Google / open-weight Qwen or Llama). Do not exceed ~5 — marginal judges add cost, not independence. Measure and report **n_eff**; if n_eff/k < 0.5, treat the panel verdict as a single noisy label and lean on human calibration.
- **Never trust the raw panel rate.** Estimate each judge's sensitivity/specificity on the human-adjudicated set, apply Rogan-Gladen (or PPI), and report bias-corrected metrics with CIs. Budget: **~200 human-adjudicated items** for a ~±0.1 CI on a headline prevalence (e.g., "% verdicts correct"); scale up if judges are weaker (sens/spec near 0.6). Reserve ~10% of total eval effort for this.
- **Version comparison = paired A/B on identical items, position-swapped.** Judge version-A vs version-B verdicts side-by-side on the same claims; run each pair twice with order swapped and average (position-consistency). Keep an absolute-score track in parallel to catch pairwise bias amplification.
- **Blinding:** strip all version/system identifiers, model names, and stylistic tells (verdict verbosity, formatting) before judging; normalize rationale length across arms so verbosity bias can't proxy for version. Blinding alone will *not* remove self/family preference.
- **Aggregation:** simple majority vote or mean is fine as the panel label; do not expect Dawid-Skene weighting to rescue correlated judges. Put the statistical rigor in the *human-calibrated correction*, not the aggregation rule.

## Pitfalls to avoid

1. **Circularity:** never let a judge from the same family as (or the same model as) the pipeline's verdict-generator into the panel — family bias compounds self-preference and survives blinding. Keep the generator's family out of the jury.
2. **Panel theater:** stacking 7–9 same-era frontier models feels rigorous but buys ~2 effective votes; unanimity is *not* strong evidence (29% all-correct / 5% all-wrong by correlation, not truth). Report n_eff, and always keep a human ground-truth anchor.
3. **Reporting raw judge rates as the headline:** uncorrected judge prevalence is systematically biased (over-estimates low true accuracy, under-estimates high). Every headline number needs bias correction + a CI that includes calibration uncertainty — and for A/B claims, remember comparison is more fragile than single-model estimation.
